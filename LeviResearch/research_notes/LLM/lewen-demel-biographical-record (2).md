# Lewen Demel ("Levi") — What the Records Establish
Compiled 13 Sep 2026 from Todd's photographs of WO 12/7088 (7th Battalion, 60th Foot, muster books and pay-lists, 1813–1815, The National Archives, Kew) and the hired researcher's PANS notes.

**Research layers:** (1) Allen B. Robertson Ph.D., Halifax — hired ~2000; Nova Scotia church-record research (report to be located in Todd's files). (2) "Ruth" (surname Bloom per Kew document tickets) — hired researcher at The National Archives, ~2019; source of the WO 12 and ADM 103 photographs and the pay-list notes. (3) Walter E. DeMelle ("WED"), Todd's uncle (d. before 2026) — decades of family research including a 2003 Nova Scotia trip; his 2019 notes synthesize the layers. Todd and Walter researched the family together.

Each item is tagged:
- **[attested]** — stated directly in a primary document in hand
- **[inferred]** — follows from the documents but not stated against his name
- **[derived]** — calculated from attested figures plus a documented rule; arithmetic shown, verification target named
- **[secondary]** — from a secondary source or the researcher's notes, not yet verified against a primary document

## Identity

- **[attested]** Name recorded as "Demel, Lewen" — private, 7th Battalion, 60th Regiment of Foot. (Dec 1813 – Mar 1814 pay-list, WO 12/7088)
- **[attested]** Later pay-lists render the name "Leven Demell," "Luen Demi," "Lewin Demel," "Lew. Demel," settling on "Leven Demell" — and in the 3rd Battalion books (1817–18), "**Louis Demel/Demell**." (Researcher's reading plus Todd's photographs; variants clerk-certified as one man by red auditor corrections and the "From the 7th Battn" annotation)
- **[attested]** Listed in Company No. 9; his line sits at running number 193 of the alphabetical privates list for the quarter. (Dec–Mar pay-list. Note: 193 is his position in that quarter's alphabetical list, not a regimental service number.)
- **[attested — conflation caution]** The battalion contained a **second Demel: "Demell/Demoll, Casimir"** — enlisted in Scotland 12 Oct 1813 (Edinburgh stream, marched via Tilbury Fort), joined the battalion 30 Dec 1813, one day after Levi's draft; appears in later Halifax lists as "Casp. Demoll." Any "Demel" found in Edinburgh/Perth POW registers is probably Casimir; only a Portsmouth-area register hit matches Lewen without further proof. (Soft flag: a "Dehlunden, Jacobus" in the Scotland detachment vs. "Deklunder, Jacob" in Levi's Portsmouth bracket — possibly two men, possibly clerical blur; unresolved.)
- Family memory: Todd's grandfather referred to him as "Levi."

## Origin (as far as the records reach so far)

- **[attested]** He was a prisoner of war held at Portsmouth immediately before enlistment. (Clerk's note, first-formation book: men "inlisted by Captn Marschalck, 1st Veteran Battn K.G. Legion, from the Prisoners of War at Portsmouth"; his entry bracketed "Vol. from Pris. of War")
- **[inferred]** He had served in Napoleon's forces and was captured by or surrendered to the British — that is what made him a POW. Which service (army or a French ship), which unit, and where captured: not yet documented; land soldier is probable but unproven (see open question 1). (Target: ADM 103 Portsmouth registers)
- **[inferred]** German or German-classified nationality. The battalion was raised specifically "from amongst the Prisoners of War who are natives of Germany" (Horse Guards letter, 2 Aug 1813, WO 1/656 — secondary transcription), and the name is Germanic. Not stated against his name in any document in hand.

## Enlistment

- **[attested]** Enlisted by Captain Marschalck of the 1st Veteran Battalion, King's German Legion, acting as recruiting officer at Portsmouth. (Clerk's enlistment note; unit reading confirmed by the Aug–Sep voucher specification: "Appd Return of 43 Recruits inlisted by Captn Marschalck, 1st Vet Battn K.G. Legion, from the Prisoners of War, for the 7th Battn 60 Regt")
- **[attested]** POW recruits were staged at **Fort Cumberland** (Eastney, Portsmouth) between enlistment and shipment — the Aug–Sep specification includes a return for extra price of meat "issued the Recruits at Fort Cumberland, 25 August to 24 Septr." **[inferred]** Levi's pre-battalion subsistence ("subsisted to 15th Decemb") most plausibly took place there — the physical location of his mid-December 1813.
- **[attested]** Every POW draft generated a named "Approved Return" voucher: the Aug–Sep quarter carries eight of them (vouchers 5–12, for 43 + 3 + 64 + 27 + 49 + 69 + 54 + 46 ≈ 355 recruits; attesting money receipted for 353). The December draft's equivalent — naming Levi — is the Dec–Mar quarter's Voucher No. 1 or its companion return.
- **[attested]** Subsistence — army pay — commenced 16 December 1813. This is the earliest date attested against his name in any record in hand. (Dec–Mar pay-list: paid 16 Dec – 24 Mar, 99 days)
- **[attested at battalion level]** The battalion's Recruiting Accompt (HQ, 25 Sep–24 Dec 1813, in WO 12/7088) records for every recruit: date of enlistment, **bounty of £4 4s (four guineas) paid to the recruit**, 1s attesting fee, £4 5s total levy money, each batch keyed by red letter to a numbered Approval Return. Levi's own bounty entry sits in the same document class — the following quarter's recruiting accompt or Marschalck's Portsmouth approval return — not yet photographed. The land-in-Canada promise (after five years' service, WO 1/656) remains attested as recruiting terms, not as an individual receipt.

## Service, Dec 1813 – Mar 1814 — full reading of his ledger line

Everything below is from his single line in the Dec–Mar pay-list (WO 12/7088), read column by column from Todd's photographs. Readings from cropped photos; verify against the original at Kew.

- **[attested]** Company column: **9** — carried on the books of No. 9 Company.
- **[attested]** Voucher column: **1** — his pay charge is referred to Voucher No. 1 of the quarter. Per the voucher specification page, Voucher 1 is the "Original Report for 120 Men subsisted to 15th Decemb: 1813, and who arrived at Head Quarters 29th Decemb." This is a documentary cross-reference tying Demel to the 120-man draft: subsisted by the recruiting service through 15 Dec, on battalion pay from 16 Dec, arrived Guernsey 29 Dec. (This strengthens the arrival date from cohort inference to a ledger cross-reference — though the voucher itself, which would list the 120 names, is the clinching document if it survives.)
- **[attested]** Higher-pay columns (14 years / 7 years service): blank — he had no length-of-service pay entitlement, i.e., no prior *British* service counted.
- **[attested]** Running number 193 in the quarter's alphabetical privates list (position in that list, not a service number). Adjacent lines: 192 Derohm, Peter (73 days, transferred to Lymington — different cohort); 194 Deklunder, Jacob and 195 Durein, Chas. — his bracket-mates, paid identically and covered by the same enlistment remark.
- **[attested]** Pay period: From 16 (Dec), To 24 (Mar), No. of Days 99. Amount issued: **£4 7s 6d**.
- **[attested]** Remarks: "as No 5" written against the muster-absence columns, bracketing him with Deklunder and Durein — pointing to the full remark at man No. 5 of the list, i.e., the Marschalck/Portsmouth POW enlistment note. Its position explains his absence from the **1st muster** of the quarter (taken ~25 Dec, while his draft was newly enlisted/in transit from Portsmouth).
- **[attested]** Checkmarks under the 2nd and 3rd musters: physically present with the battalion on Guernsey at the January and February/March musters — his first two verified appearances in ranks.
- **[attested]** Beer-money column: **76**. Beer money was a documented allowance of 1d per day, established 1800, paid to NCOs and soldiers in lieu of the daily beer issue — payable on **home service only** (abolished into daily pay 1873). 76 days × 1d = 6s 4d.
- **[attested]** Red audit annotations on the pay pages record days on **ship rations** (victualled aboard transports) against the 99-day cohort's rows: "to 29 Dec — 14" and "from 16 March — 9". The quarter reconciles exactly: 14 days at sea (16–29 Dec, Portsmouth→Guernsey passage) + 76 days ashore on Guernsey (30 Dec–15 Mar, the beer-money days) + 9 days at sea (16–24 Mar, first leg of the Atlantic crossing) = 99 days. **Embarkation for North America: 16 March 1814.** (First derived from the beer-money gap, then confirmed by the ship-rations notes, and independently corroborated on cohort-mate Auven, Franc — identical 16 Dec/99-day/76-beer/14+9 ship-ration pattern. The Lymington-transfer men are annotated "F[oreign] Depot from 8 March" — detached to the Foreign Depot a week before the battalion sailed; even the late 28 Feb draft reconciles: 5 ship-ration days for its own passage, 11 beer days, 9 at sea.)
- **[attested]** The Dec–Mar paymaster's affidavit carries a margin note that the battalion "was at sea 24 March"; the Mar–May affidavit states "The Battn was on board Ship during the whole period" (25 Mar–24 May 1814), its pay drawn in advance at Guernsey and credited in the general state to 24 March. The crossing thus ran 16 March to late May 1814 — ~10½ weeks at sea.
- **[attested]** Far column bracket: "Vol. from Pris. of War" — volunteered from prisoner of war.
- **[attested]** Battalion headquarters throughout his first weeks was Guernsey. (Sep–Dec 1813 pay-list cover: "Stationed at Guernsey"; Dec–Mar cover note: the battalion "left Guernsey" mid-quarter)

## Battalion context and movements

- **[attested]** Battalion first formed Aug–Sep 1813, stationed at Sandown (Isle of Wight), Colonel Sir George Murray, first recruits enlisted from POWs at Portsmouth from 25/30 Aug 1813 onward. He was NOT yet in the battalion; he does not appear in the first-formation list. (First-formation pay-list, 25 Aug – 24 Sep 1813)
- **[attested]** Battalion left Guernsey during the Dec 1813 – Mar 1814 quarter; the quarter's pay-list was not submitted until 20 Apr 1816, from Halifax, by Paymaster C. C. S. Worsley. (Dec–Mar cover and note)
- **[secondary]** Arrived Halifax, Nova Scotia, 28 May 1814, from Europe; departed 25 August 1814 for Castine, Maine (Penobscot expedition). (Researcher's PANS notes, Military Papers H.Q.O., microfilm reel 9216)
- **[secondary]** Battalion remained at Halifax until disbandment in 1817; men drafted into the 2nd and 3rd Battalions, 60th Foot. (PANS notes; warof1812.ca battalion history)
- **[secondary]** The battalion was trained as a light corps, dressed in rifle green. A June 1814 inspection at Halifax (per an inspection return transcribed by Chartrand, warof1812.ca) recorded: a "tolerable good body of men," their appearance "healthy & clean"; NCOs well chosen and attentive to duty, though many could not read English; almost all the men unacquainted with English, making drill difficult for the officers; and the greater part previously "drilled in the French manner," with habits from Napoleon's service that were hard to break. This is the nearest thing to an eyewitness description of Levi's cohort six months after he joined.

## Halifax quarters, 1814–1815 — continuous presence

From Todd's photographs of the successive quarterly covers and D-pages (all WO 12/7088; readings from photos, verify at Kew):

- **[attested]** Unbroken chain of pay-lists "Stationed at Halifax" with Demell present at musters, no adverse remarks: May–Jun 1814; Jun–Sep 1814 (the Castine quarter — 11 vouchers, list not sent to London until Jan 1815, received 25 Apr 1815); Sep–Dec 1814 (3 vouchers); Dec 1814–Mar 1815 (1 voucher); Mar–Jun 1815; Sep–Dec 1815 (4 vouchers). Late lists under Paymaster Worsley were transmitted in a batch, Aug 1816.
- **[attested]** Name variants tracked list by list: "Demel, Lewin"; "Leven Demell"; "Lew. Demel"; "Demi, Luen" — the last corrected in red by the auditor: "**Demell do.**" (alongside "Derider in last" correcting "Dendere"), the pay office itself certifying the variants are one man. "Levan" overwritten "Leven" in the Mar–Jun 1815 list. Settles toward "Leven Demell," as the researcher reported.
- **[attested]** Garrison context on the same pages: desertions around him (Diadeka deserted 31 Aug 1814, back on books 10 Oct; Devis deserted 29 Oct; clusters of Company 2 men "absent" at successive musters). Demell's line remains clean throughout.
- **[FLAG — verify at Kew]** Company column: 9 in the 1813–14 and mid-1814 lists; the 1815 lists appear to read **1**; the final 1817 list appears to read **10**. Nos. 1 and 10 were the two rifle flank companies per the battalion history (no company commanders' names are attested in the documents in hand — an earlier draft's "Ward's Rifle Company" conflated No. 1 Coy with Acting Pay-Master Capt. Ward and has been removed as unsupported), which would matter (Baker rifle; Maine occupation to Apr 1815) — but three values across the years means either real reshuffling as the battalion shrank, or a misread of what this column records in the later lists. Photo-readings of a cramped column; not promoted to attested. Tests: the original column headings at Kew; detachment remarks in his 1815 muster lines; Castine prize roll WO 164/561.

## Disbandment, 1817 — transfer to the 3rd Battalion

From the final quarterly list, Mar–Jun 1817 (Halifax; sent from the regiment 23 Aug 1817, after the battalion had ceased to exist; audit closed 14 Feb 1822). **Source volume: WO 12/7089 (7th Bn, 1816–1818, spine-labeled "Disbanded")** — the 7th Battalion's books split as WO 12/7088 (1813–1815) and WO 12/7089 (1816–1818); the 1816–17 quarters and disbandment papers are in 7089:

- **[attested]** The auditor's red key sorts the men four ways: subsisted to Sept 1817 on Lieut. Jellicoe's or Capt. Barrington's lists "for late 7th Bn" (discharge track), or transferred to the **3rd Battalion, 60th Regiment**, per Return vouchers 6 and 8 of the September 1817 list (two accounting variants). Detachments at the end: men "On Commd. at Cape Breton" and "On Commd. at Annapolis," absent all three musters.
- **[attested, symbol reading]** Demell's line carries the marks routing him to the **3rd Battalion** — confirming the PANS note and battalion history that the retained (largely German) men went to the 2nd/3rd Battalions. Which voucher variant (6 or 8) depends on the exact symbols; verify at Kew.
- **Research consequence:** Return vouchers 6 and 8 of the Sept 1817 list are named transfer lists that would carry his name; and the trail continues in the **3rd Battalion 60th Foot WO 12 series** (Quebec posting per battalion history) — the bridge toward his discharge and the land grant.

## 3rd Battalion, 1817–1818 — "Louis Demel," and the discharge in Nova Scotia

From Todd's photographs of the 3rd Battalion 60th (Light Infantry, Colonel Hon. Edmund Phipps) quarterly books, Halifax NS, Jun 1817 – Sep 1818 (separate WO 12 piece — number to be identified in Discovery; readings from photos):

- **[attested]** The 3rd Battalion's Jun–Sep 1817 specification lists **Voucher 6: "Order transferring Men from the 7th to the 3rd Batt — and Description Roll"** (and Voucher 8: transfers from the 2nd Batt, with its own description roll; Voucher 9: an individual 7th→3rd transfer). A **description roll** records age, height, complexion, hair/eyes, **birthplace**, and trade — if voucher 6 survives with this book, it is a second, independent route to Levi's origin, and the highest-value single target at Kew.
- **[attested]** He appears in the 3rd Battalion lists as **"Demel/Demell, Louis,"** annotated in red "**From the 7th Battn**" — clerk-certified continuity; **Company 10** (Ruth Bloom's direct reading of the Jun–Sep 1817 list). Name drift completes: Lewen → Leven → Louis. Carried at 6d per diem through the 1817–18 quarters, no adverse remarks; battalion at Halifax with detachments at Annapolis, Windsor, and Cape Breton. **[identification note]** Leven = Louis rests on the "From the 7th Battn" annotation, the unique surname in these lists, and the timing — strong but the first-name shift is assumed, not certified by a "do." correction as earlier variants were.
- **[attested]** **Discharge:** in the Jun–Sep 1818 quarter (entry #130) his line is paid 25 June – 24 July 1818 (30 days), ruled off in red, annotated "**discharged**" as of **25 July 1818**, "whither: **in this Country**" — while surrounding men are marked "Embarked — for England" as the battalion was reduced and shipped home. He took his discharge in Nova Scotia on 25 July 1818.
- **[attested]** That quarter's specification names the discharge paper: **Voucher 11, "Return & Orders of Men discharged in this Country"** — a named list including him. (Other vouchers that quarter document the reduction: detachment embarkation returns on the Transport Anne from Halifax, the Importer from Cape Breton, the Sloop Hare from Annapolis, and HM Brig Opossum.)
- **Total service:** 16 Dec 1813 – ~24 Jul 1818, four years seven months. (The land-in-Canada promise was framed as after five years' service; his discharge at ~4½ years came in a general reduction — whether and how the land entitlement was administered for such men is a question for the Nova Scotia land papers.)

## Unit history — 7th Battalion, 60th Foot in North America

All **[secondary]** unless noted — principal source: 7/60th reenactment association battalion history (7thbattalion60th.wordpress.com), corroborated in part by warof1812.ca (Raynor/Chartrand) and the PANS notes.

- Raised from POWs per the Duke of York's proposal of 2 Aug 1813 (WO 1/656): recruits "natives of Germany," bounty of four guineas, and "the eventual grant of land in Canada after a service of five years." Ultimately ~840 Germans, Dutch, Swiss, French, and Hungarians enlisted. Commanded by Lt-Col Henry John, late 95th Rifles; trained as a light corps.
- Organization: ten companies — eight centre companies with New Land Pattern light infantry muskets, two flank companies (No. 1 and No. 10) with Baker rifles. **Levi's No. 9 was a musket company** [inference from attested company number + secondary organization].
- **[attested]** The battalion's cadre came from elite light corps: the Aug–Sep voucher specification records a party from the 3rd Battalion 95th (Rifles) and NCOs from the 2nd Battalion 52nd (Light Infantry) marched in under War Office routes of Aug–Sep 1813 — matching Lt-Col John's own 95th background. Levi was trained by riflemen and light-infantry NCOs.
- **[attested]** Recruiting streams per the voucher specifications: POWs enlisted at Portsmouth by Marschalck (~355 in Aug–Sep alone; Levi's December draft of 120); men joined from **the Foreign Depot** (the foreign-recruits depot — the Lymington connection seen in the "Transf. to Lymington" annotations); and men **enlisted in Scotland** (the Scottish POW depots around Edinburgh), per the Sep–Dec specification (which also records a deserter taken, and certificates from the Deputy Paymaster-General at Guernsey).
- Arrived Halifax spring 1814 (28 May per PANS; 31 May per battalion history — minor conflict, unresolved).
- **Penobscot expedition, Sep 1814:** the flank companies joined Sherbrooke's amphibious force against Maine. Castine captured 1 Sep; Lt-Col John with 700 men (incl. No. 1 Coy) pushed up the Penobscot to destroy USS Adams and on 3 Sep defeated ~1,400 American militia at Hampden, the 60th turning the flank in fog and taking a battery. No. 10 Coy at the capture of Fort Machias, 11 Sep. British forces then held eastern Maine until the peace.
- Per the battalion history, the remainder of the battalion — which would include No. 9 Coy — garrisoned Nova Scotia (Halifax and Annapolis Royal) against seaborne raids and did NOT go to Maine. The PANS note ("departure 25 August for Castine") may describe only the expedition detachment. **Unresolved; decisive test: whether Demel appears in the Castine prize rolls, WO 164/561.**
- Rifle companies withdrew from Maine Apr 1815; battalion on the Atlantic-colonies peacetime establishment (incident: near-mutiny of the PEI militia at Charlottetown, Nov 1815, No. 6 Coy present, no bloodshed).
- Disbanded in Nova Scotia, June 1817. Most non-German personnel discharged and repatriated to Europe; the rest transferred to the 2nd and 3rd Battalions, 60th, posted to Quebec City. Levi's continued appearance in later lists (per the researcher) rather than repatriation quietly supports the German-origin inference [inferred].
- **[attested]** Named officers from the pay-list certificates and affidavits in hand: Lt-Col **Henry John**, Commanding Officer (signature on the Halifax certificate, July 1814); Paymaster **Charles C. S. Worsley** (affidavits sworn at Halifax, April 1816); Acting Pay-Master **Capt. Ward** (May–Jun 1814 list). Colonel of the regiment: Sir George Murray (covers).

## Family tradition

- Todd's grandfather referred to him as "Levi."
- Tradition holds Levi was granted land in Canada and ended up on property in Nova Scotia, possibly by trading the grant; the family line Todd knows descends from Nova Scotia.
- The land-grant element matches the battalion's documented recruiting terms (land in Canada after five years' service, WO 1/656) — the tradition preserves the actual enlistment contract. Grant location, date, and any trade are undocumented so far.

## Nova Scotia, 1818–1858 — settler at Sherbrooke/New Ross and Chester

From the notes of **Walter E. DeMelle ("WED"), Todd's uncle** (written 2019, drawing on his own 2003 Nova Scotia research and reading, and incorporating Allen B. Robertson Ph.D. research, Halifax, 2000), and J.S. Martell, "Military Settlements in Nova Scotia after the War of 1812" (NSHS *Collections*, vol. 24):

- **[secondary — Martell]** The land grant was a documented Crown program for his exact cohort: **April 1817, Dalhousie asked Bathurst for permission (received) to grant free land to ~50 Germans of the 7th Batt., 60th Regiment**, settled in Lunenburg County along the projected Halifax–Annapolis road at the military settlements around **Sherbrooke (now New Ross)**; Surveyor-General Morris to Capt. Ross, Apr 1817: expect "Many Setlers Germans and Highlanders on your Road between Gold River and Annapolis." Martell p. 93 describes the fifty as having "fought with Wellington in Europe and with Sherbrooke at Castine." (Note his April 1817 date precedes the June 1817 disbandment; Levi served to Jul 1818 — whether he was in the original fifty or granted on discharge is for the land papers.)
- **[secondary — Robertson 2000, cited]** **The grant itself, indexed:** NS Archives **RG 20 "A" (Post-1800 Land Grant Papers): "DeMill, Levi — see: Ross, William (Lieut.) & Others 1822"** — Draft Grant to military settlers at Sherbrooke Settlement, 6,900 acres in Lunenburg and Kings Counties (grantee list includes German names: Kiens, Graff, Clotiz, Shipain, Hession): **Levi DeMill — 200 acres.** Martell p. 102: this second grant was made up 1821, **effective January 1822**, 45 grantees, and "happily names the corps to which each man formerly belonged" — so the grant document itself states his regiment. (This is the documentation Walter recalled seeing.)
- **[family recollection — WED]** WED recalls having personally seen documentation that Levi was a land-grant recipient (details not recorded).
- **[secondary — Dimock diary, p. 123]** **20 Feb 1821: marriage of "Levi Demal of Sherbrook" to Elizabeth Refus of Chester**, by Rev. Joseph Dimock (Baptist) — placing Levi resident at the military settlement three years after discharge.
- **[secondary — Robertson 2000, St. Stephen's Anglican Chester, Micro 11282-X, p. 38]** **18 Apr 1826: "Leven Demel," widower, of St. Stephen's Parish Chester, married Mary Ann Wilneuf/Willineuf** of the same place, by banns, James Shreve rector; witnesses John Connet, Joseph Lorelly; **both signed with X marks** (illiterate — consistent with the four decades of clerk-spelled name variants). Elizabeth d. between 1821 and 1826.
- **[secondary — Robertson, same register, p. 33]** Son **Peter Levi**, born 1 Jun 1831, baptized Mar 1833, "son of Levi and Mary Ann Dimmels, Chester, **Farmer**."
- **[secondary — Robertson]** Life dates: **Levi/Leven Demel 1784–1858; Mary Ann 1784–1863.** Birth ~1784 → age ~29 at enlistment (fits the POW-register cohort ages). **Conflict: Todd's 2019 query to the Kew researcher states Levi "died in Nova Scotia in 1837"** — two family-sourced death years, 1837 vs 1858, both unverified; the burial-register search must test both. Underlying source for Robertson's dates not stated — verify.
- **[family lore — Todd, 2019 correspondence]** The granted plot was traded "so that Levi, being a **fisherman**, could be by the coast" — a second occupation tradition alongside the 1833 register's "farmer" (not incompatible: coastal smallholders commonly did both). The 1864 Rumsey map of Lunenburg County shows **"DeMill Rd."** with residences of Levi and a **Francis DeMill** (relationship unestablished — candidate son/relative to trace); the road name persists today.
- **[family lore — Walter DeMelle Sr., via Todd]** Levi "may have been from **Holland**" (paired with an impossible Boer War claim, so low weight — but the battalion demonstrably contained Dutch recruits, and the origin question is properly German-*or*-Dutch, not German only). Per Walter: the family consistently held to "d-i-m-m-e-l-l" spellings ("Dimmell — D, i, double m, e, double l," per his grandfather); DeMelle adopted on emigration to the US; Nova Scotia cousins spell DeMille.
- **Name arc completes:** Demel → Demal → Demiel → **Dimmels → Dimmell → DeMelle**.
- **[context — WED]** WED (having read Martell and the Dalhousie Journals vols. 1–2) notes the settlements mixed veterans of Europe and the American war; the 2019 researcher's suggestion that grants went only to those who fought in Europe is not supported.

## Ancillary research notes (from the 2019 photograph set)

- **False trail closed — the other "7th Battalion" of Germans:** the 7th **Line** Battalion, King's German Legion, served in the Mediterranean (Sicily; detachments "on Command at Ponza") — a completely different unit from the 7th Battalion 60th Foot. Its photographed 1814 pay-lists show no Demel among the D-surname privates (Demkes, Demm, Demure present). Recorded to prevent conflation of the two units in any future search.
- **Foreign Depot pay-lists exist:** a "Depot Bexhill District" quarterly pay-list (25 Jun–24 Sep 1813, Paymaster James Watson, 89 vouchers) was photographed — the Foreign Depot's own WO 12-class books. A possible location for records of Levi's pre-battalion subsistence (to 15 Dec 1813), alongside the recruiting-service accounts.
- **Colonial thread opened:** CO 381/62 (Colonial Office register of Nova Scotia patents, commissions, and warrants — Sherbrooke 1816, Dalhousie 1820, etc.) was photographed; nothing Levi-specific in the captured openings, but it marks the Nova Scotia-side series at Kew adjacent to the land-records search.
